# Personas

No interviews or field observations are documented yet. Every persona below is therefore a **hypothesis to validate**. The descriptions focus on roles and needs rather than fictional biographies.

## Primary roles

### Game capture operator — hypothesis to validate

Records plays during a live game and resolves incomplete observations later. Needs fast touch entry, a visible offline state, safe correction, unknown-player handling, and confidence that local data is not mistaken for server-confirmed data. Success means keeping pace with the game and producing a reviewable play log without double entry.

### Lead statistician or reviewer — hypothesis to validate

Checks the play sequence, statistical attribution, and unresolved issues before results become final. Needs an audit trail, ruleset and statistical-policy visibility, explicit inconsistency reports, and controlled reopening of finalized games. Success means approving results that can be explained and reconstructed.

### Statistics service provider — hypothesis to validate

Operates across clients, competitions, and games and may be an early buyer. Needs consistent workflows, organization isolation, reusable roster import, expected reports, reliable support, and controlled publication. Success means delivering more games with predictable quality and less post-game re-entry.

## Buyer and consumer roles

### Club, university, or league administrator — hypothesis to validate

Chooses or funds the service and governs access. Needs understandable costs, season history, competition-specific configuration, exportability, privacy controls, and confidence in match-day operation. A league purchase may require collective approval and is likely slower than a club or provider pilot.

### Coach — hypothesis to validate

Uses validated game and player statistics for review. Needs clear definitions, drill-down to supporting plays, and timely access. Coaching access and acceptable metrics may vary by competition; the role must not be assumed to control official validation.

### Media, player, or spectator — hypothesis to validate

Consumes public score and statistics views. Needs simple mobile access and a clear distinction between provisional and validated results. This audience may create reach without being the paying customer.

## Research priorities

Validate which role feels the problem most acutely, who owns the current report, who can authorize a pilot, and who pays. Observe how operators handle an unknown player, missed play, penalty, correction, network loss, and final approval. Separate requested features from workflow evidence, and do not treat the project owner's coaching experience or access to contacts as confirmed adoption.
